The craft of making things people actually finish reading

EVElena Voss · 9 min read

Every great article begins with a promise, and every reader is quietly keeping score. The opening paragraph does not need to be clever. It needs to be honest about where the next ten minutes are going.

Attention is not a resource you capture, it is one you earn in small increments. Each paragraph either pays back the effort the reader just spent or quietly spends their trust.

Good structure is invisible. Bad structure is the only thing you remember.

Rhythm before ideas

Long sentences need short ones nearby. A page of uniform paragraphs reads like a wall, even when each line is perfect. Vary the length, vary the weight, and leave room to breathe.

When readers lose their place they leave. Give them handholds: a clear subhead, a surprising number, a single sentence that stands alone and says exactly what it means.

Respect the finish line

Tell people how far they have come. A small progress cue turns an open ended scroll into a journey with an end, and that single change is often worth more than another round of editing.

End when you are done. Not a paragraph later.
