In praise of the slow web

The slow web

Somewhere along the way we decided that faster was always better. Pages loaded in milliseconds, feeds refreshed themselves, and yet nobody felt any calmer.

The slow web is not about speed at all. It is about giving people room to read, to think, and to leave without being chased.

Why it matters

Attention is the scarcest thing we own. Every interface that respects it earns trust the way a good bookshop does: quietly, over many visits.

A calm product is not an empty product. It is one that knows what to leave out.

When we removed three notifications, session length fell and satisfaction rose. Nobody missed them.

Small habits

Write the headline last. Delete the first paragraph. Ask what a person needs in the next ten seconds, and nothing more.

Treat whitespace as a feature rather than a failure to fill it.

What comes next

Expect more tools that slow down on purpose: longer drafts, gentler feeds and settings that default to quiet.

The best ones will feel like a good conversation: unhurried, and worth finishing.
